# A note from the author

I wrote this book for the moment when you realize something has changed and nobody has told you what to do next. A few years ago, I lost my mother. Before that, I lived through the decisions my parents had to make about her care, and afterward I watched my dad slowly work out how to live alone. This is the book I wish our family had had.

Most advice for caregivers is either too general to act on or too long to read when you're exhausted. This book tries to be neither. Every chapter ends with something you can do today, and you don't have to read it in order. Start wherever your family is right now.

# How this book works

This book takes you through the first 30 days of becoming your parent's care coordinator, then through the 60 days after that.

Every chapter has the same six parts, so you always know where you are:

1. **The frustration:** the moment you are probably in right now.
2. **What may be happening:** the pattern behind it.
3. **What to do now:** concrete steps, with day ranges.
4. **Exactly what to say:** scripts you can read aloud.
5. **A tool:** a checklist, worksheet, or printable you can use today.
6. **When professional help is appropriate:** who to call, and when.

Start at Chapter 1 and follow the days, or jump to the chapter that matches the problem in front of you. The days are a guide, not a deadline.

Each chapter also includes a short section called **One family's story**. The families you'll meet there (Dana and her father, Frank; Marcus and his mother, Ruth; and sisters Elena and Sofia Reyes and their mother, Carmen) are composites. Each is built from situations caregivers commonly describe. None is a real person, and any resemblance is coincidental. They're here so you can see the steps in this book play out in an ordinary, imperfect family.
